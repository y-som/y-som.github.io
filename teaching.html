Broadly, I think about schemes like quadrics and affine models of projective spaces, questions about representability and A1-naiveness, classical problems about vector bundles and torsors, and computations using some combination of Sage, Macaulay2, and Mathematica.

research

Writing to be uploaded here in the future. For now, a summary.

Let D be a split division algebra over R (i.e. apply the Cayley-Dickson construction to R with a plus +1). These give algebras, denoted C, H, and O whach are R^2, Mat_2(R), and (Mat_2(R))^2 with a twisted multiplication.

We define a projective space P^n(D) by considering "rank one projection matrices" in Herm_(n+1)(D). These are familiar (and of interest for various reasons). The P^1(D) are the familiar quadrics Q_2, Q_4, and Q_8. P^n(C) are Jouanolou devices for P^n. For P^n(H), there are some beautiful results due to Panin and Walter.

My thesis is about octonionic projective space. Some partial progress has resulted in explicit vector bundle torsors exhibiting the contractibility of subvarieties of Q_2n. These were used by Asok, Doran, and Fasel to show the Q_2n are motivic spheres.
