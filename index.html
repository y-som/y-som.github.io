Research

I work in motivic homotopy theory. My research interests include affine models of projective spaces such as quadrics; representability and A^1-naiveness; and classical problems about vector bundles and torsors. In addition, I am interested in applying computational tools like Sage, Macaulay2, and Mathematica to these areas.

My thesis concerns octonionic projective space. Let D be a split division algebra over R (i.e. apply the Cayley-Dickson construction to R with a plus +1). These give algebras, denoted C, H, and O whach are R^2, Mat_2(R), and (Mat_2(R))^2 with a twisted multiplication.

We define a projective space P^n(D) by considering "rank one projection matrices" in Herm_(n+1)(D). These are familiar (and of interest for various reasons). The P^1(D) are the familiar quadrics Q_2, Q_4, and Q_8. P^n(C) are Jouanolou devices for the usual P^n. For P^n(H), there are some beautiful results due to Panin and Walter (2010).

I am particularly interested in octonionic projective space, P^n(O) (which meaningfully exists only for n=1,2). Some partial progress has resulted in explicit vector bundle torsors exhibiting the contractibility of subvarieties of Q_2n. These were used by Asok, Doran, and Fasel (2014) to show the Q_2n are motivic spheres. In addition, some (surprising) negative results limiting how P^2(O) can be realized as a cofiber of the motivic Hopf map. A non-technical summary of my thesis results can be read here.
